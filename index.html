The ground moves under you

Your territory is wherever your data lives and someone’s rules apply. Most of it belongs to someone else, who can change those rules or lock you out. Three real cases.

  • Locked out

    In May 2025, AP reported that the chief prosecutor of the International Criminal Court lost access to his Microsoft email after US sanctions. He moved his email to Proton. Microsoft disputes that it ended services to the court.

    Source: Associated Press, 15 May 2025. Microsoft’s denial: heise online, 5 June 2025.

  • The rules change under you

    In February 2024, Atlassian ended support for the Jira and Confluence that teams ran on their own servers. No more support, no more bug fixes. Teams could move to Atlassian’s cloud, buy a different licence, or carry on alone. Owning the server did not mean owning the decision.

    Source: Atlassian, Server end of support.

  • AI on your data

    In May 2024, people read Slack’s privacy principles closely. Slack analysed customer data, messages and files included, to build its own machine learning models. Every workspace was in by default. To opt out, an admin had to email Slack. Slack then said these models only see de-identified, aggregate data.

    Source: Computerworld, 20 May 2024.

What leaving means

In the EU you can take your phone number to another provider, and people still reach you. Leaving an online service should work the same way.

  • Your data

    Everything you created, complete, in open formats another service can read.

  • Your identity

    The key you sign in with goes with you, so people and systems still know it is you.

  • Your setup

    Your groups, roles and rules, rebuilt on the other side instead of typed in again.

The law points the same way

  1. 12 Sep 2025The EU Data Act applies, including its rules for switching cloud services.
  2. 12 Jan 2027Providers may no longer charge you for switching.

The law moves data. It does not move your identity, or the proof of what happened to your data. That part is ours to build.

Where we stand: your sign-in key is already yours, and you can download what you store with us. Moving your whole setup in one step, and a public exit drill that proves it, are still ahead of us.

What we do differently

Claiming ground is the start. Defending it means you can leave, you can prove what happened, and you never depend on one operator, us included.

  • We run it, you hold the keys

    We operate the servers. The keys that prove who you are stay with you. Running your tools does not mean holding your secrets.

  • Leaving is a feature

    Data, identity and setup are built to move together. We will test that in public exit drills and publish what broke.

  • Proof you can check

    Your files, signed documents and meeting transcripts carry signed proof of who changed what, and when. Anyone can check it with open tools, without asking us.

  • Open, so someone else can take over

    Open code and open standards. Our sign-in service and the developer kits are public today. Some service code is still private; we are opening it, and the service list says where each one stands.

What we don’t do

  • No ads, no data sales, no training on your data.
  • No exit fees, no proprietary formats.
  • No access to your keys, unless a service needs it. Then we say so, service by service.
  • No token, no coin. Timestamps are anchored on Ethereum, and operators pay that cost. No token exists and none is planned.
  • We don’t make you a sysadmin.

Build with Aqua

Our own territory map

We hold ourselves to the same map. This site runs on GitHub Pages today. Here is who holds each layer of it, why, and how we would leave.

  1. IdentityWho are you? Ours, at a registrar we can change

    switchways.org is registered with United Domains, and we can move it to another registrar.

  2. DataWhat did you create? Ours

    The source lives in a GitHub repository, and every one of our working copies holds all of it.

  3. RulesWho may do what? Ours

    We decide what gets published. GitHub’s terms still apply to the hosting.

  4. GroundWhere does it run? Theirs: GitHub Pages

    GitHub, a Microsoft company, serves these pages and logs visitors’ IP addresses for security.

  5. IntelligenceWho thinks for you? Not used

    Nothing on this site thinks for you.

Why GitHub Pages

The site is plain files. Pages serves them reliably at no cost and leaves us no web server to patch. Nothing you give us passes through it: the forms send to our own service.

How we would leave

  1. Copy the built files to another host, or to a server of our own. Nothing in them depends on GitHub.
  2. Point the domain at the new host.
  3. Keep the forms as they are: they already talk to our own service.

Your browser talks to two places here: this site and our own form service. No fonts, analytics or embeds from anyone else.

One more honest line: our company email, info@inblock.io, still runs on Google Workspace. When we reply by email, Google carries that mail.
